Greek Numbers 1-10, Days of the Week and Telling the Time

Learn Greek numbers 1-10, the days of the week and how to tell the time in Greek, with pronunciation and easy memory tricks.

Numbers, days and time appear in almost every real conversation: booking a table, catching a ferry, arranging a lesson. These are great words to learn early.

A memory trick: Δευτέρα means “second”, Τρίτη “third”, Τετάρτη “fourth” and Πέμπτη “fifth”. The week counts from Sunday as the first day, so the days line up with the numbers.

Telling the time

Ask Τι ώρα είναι; (ti ora ine), “What time is it?”. The answer starts with Είναι... (ine) followed by the hour. The exception is one o’clock, which uses the feminine μία.

GreekSay it likeMeaning
Είναι μία.ine miaIt is one o’clock.
Είναι δύο.ine dhioIt is two o’clock.
Είναι τρεις.ine trisIt is three o’clock.
Είναι τέσσερις και μισή.ine tesseris ke misiIt is half past four.
Είναι πέντε και τέταρτο.ine pende ke tetartoIt is quarter past five.
Είναι έξι παρά τέταρτο.ine exi para tetartoIt is quarter to six.

Practise it

Try saying out loud what day it is today, what time it is now, and when your next appointment is. Small daily habits like this turn lists into real knowledge. When you are ready, a tutor can help you book, plan and chat about your week in Greek.

Frequently asked questions

How do you count to ten in Greek?

Ένα, δύο, τρία, τέσσερα, πέντε, έξι, επτά, οκτώ, εννέα, δέκα (ena, dhio, tria, tesera, pende, exi, efta, okhto, enia, dheka).

What are the days of the week in Greek?

Δευτέρα, Τρίτη, Τετάρτη, Πέμπτη, Παρασκευή, Σάββατο and Κυριακή, from Monday to Sunday.

How do you ask the time in Greek?

Say Τι ώρα είναι; (ti ora ine). The answer starts with Είναι, for example Είναι τρεις (it is three o'clock).
