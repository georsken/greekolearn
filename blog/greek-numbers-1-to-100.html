Greek Numbers 1 to 100: A Simple Guide with Pronunciation

VocabularyPublished 16 April 20262 min read

Numbers are one of the quickest wins in Greek. Learn ten words, then a few patterns, and you can count to a hundred and understand prices in shops and cafés.

11 to 19

Eleven and twelve are special: έντεκα (EN-de-ka) and δώδεκα (THOH-the-ka). From 13 onwards, join δεκα- with the unit: δεκατρία (13), δεκατέσσερα (14), δεκαπέντε (15), δεκαέξι (16), δεκαεφτά (17), δεκαοχτώ (18), δεκαεννιά (19).

Putting numbers together

Between 21 and 99 you say the ten followed by the unit, with no "and". So 21 is είκοσι ένα, 35 is τριάντα πέντε and 99 is ενενήντα εννιά. Notice the pattern: the tens from 30 to 90 mostly end in -άντα or -ήντα, which makes them easy to spot.

Numbers in real life

  • Prices: Πόσο κάνει; Δώδεκα ευρώ. (How much is it? Twelve euros.)
  • Age: Είμαι τριάντα πέντε χρονών. (I am 35 years old.)
  • Phone numbers: Greeks often say them digit by digit or in pairs.
  • Time: see our guide to days, months and telling the time.
Gender matters for 1, 3 and 4. Say ένας φίλος (one male friend), μία φίλη (one female friend) and ένα παιδί (one child). From 5 up, numbers do not change.

How to memorise them

Count the steps you take, read the numbers on buses, and say the price of everything you buy. Ten minutes a day for a week is enough to count to 100 without thinking.
