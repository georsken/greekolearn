How Long Does It Take to Learn Greek? Realistic Timelines

Getting startedPublished 9 June 20262 min read

The honest answer depends on your goal and how often you practise. Here are realistic ranges, so you can plan without false promises.

Think in hours, not months

The U.S. Foreign Service Institute estimates that English speakers need roughly 1,100 hours of study to reach professional working proficiency in Greek. That is a high bar, and most learners have a much more modest goal. The table below gives rough, practical ranges, assuming focused study with speaking practice. Everyone is different.

GoalLevel (CEFR)Approximate study time
Greet people, order food, ask directionsA150 to 100 hours (about 2 to 4 months at 4 to 5 hours a week)
Handle everyday situations and simple chatsA2150 to 250 hours (about 6 to 9 months)
Hold conversations on familiar topics, read simple textsB1350 to 500 hours (about 1 to 1.5 years)
Discuss most topics with confidenceB2600 to 800 hours (about 2 years)
Near-professional fluencyC1 and above1,000+ hours
These are estimates, not guarantees. Learners with a tutor, daily practice and regular contact with Greek often move faster than the table suggests.

What speeds you up

  • Speaking from the start. Conversation is the fastest way to turn knowledge into skill.
  • Daily contact. Short daily sessions beat long weekly ones because memory needs repetition.
  • A clear goal. Travel Greek, family Greek and exam Greek need different vocabulary.
  • Immersion. Living in Greece or Cyprus, or even changing your phone to Greek, adds hours without extra study time.
  • Feedback. A tutor corrects errors before they become habits.

What slows you down

  • Putting off speaking until you feel “ready”
  • Collecting resources instead of using them
  • Studying in long, irregular bursts
  • Skipping pronunciation, which makes you harder to understand and harder to follow

A simple weekly plan (about 5 hours)

DayActivityTime
MondayNew vocabulary (10 to 15 words in phrases) and review30 min
TuesdayFree lesson or grammar topic, plus exercises45 min
WednesdayListening: a short video, podcast or song, then repeat aloud30 min
ThursdaySpeaking practice or a tutor lesson60 min
FridayReading: signs, menus, short texts, read aloud30 min
SaturdayReview the week and write 5 sentences45 min
SundayFun: watch a Greek film or listen to music60 min

What is realistic in 90 days?

With around 30 minutes a day, you can read the alphabet fluently, know a few hundred useful words, introduce yourself, order food and handle simple travel situations. It is a great goal for a first trip. See our guides to the alphabet and travel phrases to start.
