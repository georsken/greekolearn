Greek Verbs in the Present Tense: A Beginner's Guide

GrammarPublished 1 August 20262 min read

The Greek present tense is friendlier than it looks. Verbs change their endings for each person, which means you can usually drop the pronouns and still be clear.

First conjugation: γράφω (I write)

Most Greek verbs follow this pattern. Learn it well and you can conjugate hundreds of verbs.

PersonGreekPronunciation
I (εγώ)γράφωGHRAH-fo
you (εσύ)γράφειςGHRAH-fees
he/she/itγράφειGHRAH-fee
we (εμείς)γράφουμεGHRAH-fou-me
you pl./formal (εσείς)γράφετεGHRAH-fe-te
they (αυτοί)γράφουν(ε)GHRAH-foun(e)

The same pattern works for θέλω (I want), μένω (I stay, live), παίρνω (I take) and many others.

Second conjugation: αγαπώ (I love)

A second group of verbs, often ending in -ώ or -άω, has a slightly different pattern:

PersonGreekPronunciation
Iαγαπώ / αγαπάωa-gha-PO
youαγαπάςa-gha-PAS
he/she/itαγαπά / αγαπάειa-gha-PA
weαγαπάμεa-gha-PA-me
you pl./formalαγαπάτεa-gha-PA-te
theyαγαπούν(ε)a-gha-POUN(e)

Common verbs in this group: μιλάω (I speak), ρωτάω (I ask), περπατάω (I walk) and χρησιμοποιώ (I use).

The two most important verbs: είμαι and έχω

Personείμαι (I am)έχω (I have)
Iείμαιέχω
youείσαιέχεις
he/she/itείναιέχει
weείμαστεέχουμε
you pl./formalείστεέχετε
theyείναιέχουν

Example sentences: Είμαι από την Αγγλία. (I am from England.) Έχω δύο αδέλφια. (I have two siblings.)

Dropping the pronoun

Because the ending shows who is acting, Greeks usually leave out εγώ, εσύ and so on. Say Μιλάω ελληνικά, not Εγώ μιλάω ελληνικά. Add the pronoun only for emphasis: Εγώ μιλάω ελληνικά, εσύ όχι!

Negatives and questions

  • Negative: put δεν before the verb. Δεν καταλαβαίνω. (I don't understand.)
  • Question: keep the same word order and raise your voice. Μιλάς ελληνικά; (Do you speak Greek?) The Greek question mark looks like an English semicolon (;).

Talking about the future

The simplest future uses θα plus the verb: Θα πάω στην Ελλάδα. (I will go to Greece.) A form like θα γράφω expresses a repeated future action, while another stem (θα γράψω) is used for a single one. This is the aspect system you will meet later, so use the simple form for now.

Start with fifteen verbs: είμαι, έχω, θέλω, μπορώ, πηγαίνω/πάω, κάνω, μιλάω, ξέρω, βλέπω, τρώω, πίνω, μένω, δουλεύω, αγαπώ, μαθαίνω. With these you can say a surprising amount.

Pair verbs with noun cases and you can build real sentences. Practise them in a conversation with a tutor, who can correct endings as you speak.
