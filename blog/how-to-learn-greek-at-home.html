How to Learn Greek at Home: A Practical 30-Day Plan

Study tipsPublished 19 August 20262 min read

You do not need to live in Greece to make steady progress. With about 30 minutes a day, a clear plan and a bit of speaking practice, you can build a real foundation in a month.

Set a clear goal first

“Learn Greek” is too vague. Choose something specific, such as “hold a five-minute chat with my partner's family” or “order dinner on my trip in June”. Your goal decides what you learn first.

The 30-day plan

Week 1: Alphabet and sounds

  • Days 1 to 3: learn the 24 letters. Use our alphabet plan.
  • Days 4 to 5: learn the vowel pairs and the tricky consonants from the pronunciation guide.
  • Days 6 to 7: read 20 simple words aloud, then review.

Week 2: Survival phrases

  • Learn greetings and polite phrases.
  • Learn numbers to 20.
  • Practise introducing yourself: name, where you are from, what you do.

Week 3: Useful verbs and sentences

  • Study είμαι, έχω, θέλω and μπορώ from the verbs guide.
  • Build five sentences a day about your own life.
  • Learn food and travel words, using ordering in Greek.

Week 4: Speak and review

  • Have at least two real conversations, with a tutor or language partner.
  • Review everything with spaced repetition.
  • Write a short paragraph about your day and ask someone to correct it.

A 30-minute daily routine

MinutesWhat to do
5Review yesterday's words with flashcards (spaced repetition)
10Learn one new thing: 8 to 10 words in phrases, or one grammar point
10Listen to Greek and repeat out loud (shadowing)
5Say or write 3 sentences using what you learned

Tools that help

  • Flashcards with spaced repetition (Anki or similar) for vocabulary, ideally with full phrases and audio.
  • Greek subtitles on shows and films once you can read the alphabet.
  • Songs: look up the lyrics, read along and sing. It trains rhythm and stress.
  • Your phone's language setting: switch some apps to Greek for free daily exposure.
  • Our free beginner course for a structured start.

Do not skip speaking

Studying alone gets you part of the way, but speaking is a different skill. Many learners can read and recognise words, yet freeze in conversation. Book regular speaking time early, even when your Greek is basic. A native tutor can fix pronunciation, give you phrases that sound natural and keep you motivated.

Make it a habit: attach study to something you already do, such as your morning coffee. Consistency beats intensity every time.
