Greek Tutor vs Apps: The Best Way to Learn Greek Online

Greek tutor or app? Compare self-study with online Greek lessons and learn the best way for English speakers to start speaking Greek.

By GreekoLearn·30 September 2026·1 min read

In this article

  1. What self-study does well
  2. What a native tutor adds
  3. The best combination
  4. How to choose a tutor
  5. Frequently asked questions

Language apps and free courses are brilliant for getting started. But there is one skill you cannot fully train alone: speaking with a real person. Here is how the two fit together.

What self-study does well

  • Teaches the alphabet, core words and basic grammar at your own pace.
  • Costs little or nothing and fits into spare minutes.
  • Builds a daily habit with short, repeatable sessions.

What a native tutor adds

  • Real conversation. You practise understanding natural speed and replying on the spot.
  • Instant feedback. A tutor hears your pronunciation and corrects it before bad habits stick, especially on sounds like γ, χ and θ.
  • Lessons built around you. Travel, family, work or exams. You learn the Greek you will actually use.
  • Accountability. A booked lesson is much harder to skip than an app reminder.
  • Culture. Greek as people really speak it, including phrases, humour and customs no textbook explains.

The best combination

  1. Start free. Learn the alphabet and basic phrases with a free course.
  2. Add a tutor early. Even one lesson a week turns passive knowledge into speaking confidence.
  3. Practise between lessons. Ten minutes a day of vocabulary and listening makes lessons far more productive.
  4. Use Greek in real life. Songs, films, menus and messages to friends keep it fresh.

Make your tutor lessons count: tell your tutor your goal, send a few words you want to use, and ask them to correct your mistakes during the conversation, not after.

How to choose a tutor

Look for a native speaker who listens, explains clearly and adapts to your level. A trial lesson is the best test: you should leave feeling understood, a little challenged and keen to book again.

Frequently asked questions

Is a tutor better than an app for learning Greek?

Apps are great for vocabulary and daily habits. A native tutor adds real conversation, pronunciation feedback and lessons built around your goals. Most learners progress fastest by combining both.

How often should I have Greek lessons?

One or two lessons a week works well for steady progress, with ten minutes of daily practice in between.
