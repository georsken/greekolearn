How to Learn the Greek Alphabet in One Weekend

Alphabet & readingPublished 3 February 20263 min read

The Greek alphabet has 24 letters, and you already know about half of them from English, maths or science. With a clear plan, you can read Greek by Sunday evening.

The full Greek alphabet

Here are all 24 letters with their names and the closest English sound. Practise saying the name of each letter aloud.

LetterNameSound
Α αάλφα (alfa)a as in father
Β ββήτα (vita)v as in van
Γ γγάμα (gamma)soft y before e/i; a gentle gh elsewhere
Δ δδέλτα (delta)th as in this
Ε εέψιλον (epsilon)e as in bed
Ζ ζζήτα (zita)z as in zoo
Η ηήτα (ita)ee as in see
Θ θθήτα (thita)th as in think
Ι ιγιώτα (yiota)ee as in see
Κ κκάπα (kapa)k as in kite
Λ λλάμδα (lamda)l as in love
Μ μμι (mi)m as in mother
Ν ννι (ni)n as in name
Ξ ξξι (ksi)ks as in box
Ο οόμικρον (omikron)o as in more
Π ππι (pi)p as in pen
Ρ ρρο (ro)rolled or tapped r
Σ σ/ςσίγμα (sigma)s as in sun (ς only at the end of a word)
Τ τταυ (taf)t as in top
Υ υύψιλον (ipsilon)ee as in see
Φ φφι (fi)f as in fun
Χ χχι (hi)h as in a harsh “hi”, like Scottish loch
Ψ ψψι (psi)ps as in lapse
Ω ωωμέγα (omega)o as in more

Day one: Saturday

Morning: the friendly twelve

Start with letters that look and sound like English: Α, Β (careful, it sounds like v), Ε, Ζ, Ι, Κ, Μ, Ν, Ο, Τ, Φ, Ω. Write each one five times while saying its sound.

Afternoon: the lookalikes

These letters are the real traps because they look like English letters but sound different:

  • Η looks like H but sounds like "ee".
  • Ρ looks like P but is an "r".
  • Υ looks like Y but sounds like "ee".
  • Χ looks like X but is a "h/kh" sound.
  • ν (lowercase nu) looks like a v but is an "n".
  • Π is a "p", and Σ is an "s".

Evening: read your first words

Read these out loud: ταξί (taxi), μουσική (music), τηλέφωνο (telephone), σούπερ μάρκετ (supermarket), καφές (coffee). Greek is full of words you can already understand.

Day two: Sunday

Morning: the new shapes

Learn the letters with no English twin: Γ, Δ, Θ, Λ, Ξ, Π, Σ, Φ, Ψ, Ω. Use mnemonics. For example, Δ (delta) is a triangle, and Ψ looks like a trident.

Afternoon: the letter pairs

Some pairs make a single sound. Learn these seven and you can read nearly everything:

PairSoundExample
ει, οι, υιeeείμαι (ime)
αιe as in bedκαι (ke)
ουoo as in foodουζο (oozo)
μπb at the start of a wordμπίρα (bira)
ντd at the start of a wordντομάτα (domata)
γκg at the start of a wordγκάζι (gazi)
τσts as in catsτσάι (tsai)

Evening: test yourself

Read street signs, menus and the Greek word list on our free course page. Cover the pronunciation and try to say each word first.

Don't worry about accents yet. The little mark over a vowel (ά, έ, ή) only tells you which syllable to stress. Just stress that syllable and keep going.

What to do next

After the weekend, spend ten minutes a day reading signs or short texts out loud. Then move on to essential travel phrases and the pronunciation guide to polish the sounds.
