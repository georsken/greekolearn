Greek Pronunciation Guide for English Speakers

PronunciationPublished 31 March 20263 min read

Greek spelling is far more regular than English. Learn about a dozen sound rules, and you can read any word correctly. The tricky part is training your mouth for a few sounds English does not have.

Five vowel sounds

Greek has just five vowel sounds, and they stay short and clean. Unlike English, they do not glide into other sounds.

SoundSpelledLike English
aαfather
eε, αιbed
eeη, ι, υ, ει, οι, υιsee
oο, ωmore (short)
ooουfood

That means η, ι, υ, ει and οι all sound the same. It looks like a lot of work, but it makes listening easier because you only have five sounds to catch.

The tricky consonants

γ (gamma)

Before ε or ι it sounds like the English "y" in "yes": γεια (yah). Before other vowels it is a soft "gh", made like a g but without fully closing the throat: γάλα (GHAH-la, "milk").

χ (chi)

A breathy "h" sound, like the "ch" in Scottish "loch". Before ε and ι it is softer, like the "h" in "huge". Try χαρά (hah-RAH, "joy") and χέρι (HEH-ree, "hand").

θ (theta) and δ (delta)

These are the two English "th" sounds, which is good news because you already have them. θ is as in "think": θάλασσα (THAH-la-sa, "sea"). δ is as in "this": δρόμος (THRO-mos, "road").

ρ (rho)

Greek ρ is a light tap or roll of the tongue against the ridge behind your teeth, like the "tt" in American "butter". Say ρολόι (ro-LO-ee, "clock") slowly.

β, ζ, ξ, ψ

β is a "v" (βιβλίο, vee-VLEE-o, "book"), ζ a "z", ξ a "ks" and ψ a "ps". English speakers can say "ps" at the end of words (lapse), so practise placing it at the start: ψωμί (pso-MEE, "bread").

Double sounds

  • μπ sounds like "b" at the start of a word (μπάνιο, BAH-nyo) and like "mb" in the middle (τάμπλετ).
  • ντ sounds like "d" at the start (ντομάτα, do-MAH-ta) and "nd" in the middle.
  • γκ is "g" at the start and "ng" in the middle.
  • τσ is "ts", and τζ is "dj" as in jam.

Stress: the accent mark

Modern Greek uses a single accent mark (΄) to show the stressed syllable in every word with more than one syllable. Stress is important, so pronounce it clearly. Πόσο (POH-so, "how much") and ποσό (po-SO, "amount") are different words with the same letters.

Practice routine

  1. Shadow native speakers. Play a short audio clip and repeat it at the same speed and melody.
  2. Record yourself. Compare your recording with a native speaker.
  3. Drill minimal pairs. Practise words that differ by one sound, such as θέλω (I want) and τέλος (end).
  4. Slow down. Greek is spoken quickly, but your accuracy matters more than speed at the start.
  5. Get feedback. A tutor can hear errors that you cannot, and fix them in minutes.
Quick win: do not turn Greek vowels into English diphthongs. Say ναι (neh) as a short clean sound, not “nay-ee”.
