Is Greek Hard to Learn? An Honest Guide for English Speakers

Is Greek hard to learn for English speakers? See what is easy, what is tricky, how long it takes and the smartest way to start.

By GreekoLearn·9 October 2026·2 min read

In this article

  1. What is easier than you think
  2. What takes more time
  3. How long does it take?
  4. A smart way to start
  5. Frequently asked questions

The honest answer: Greek is a medium-hard language for English speakers. It is harder than Spanish or Italian, but it is far more learnable than most people expect, and some parts are surprisingly easy. Here is what to expect.

What is easier than you think

  • The alphabet. Just 24 letters, and you can learn them in a weekend.
  • Pronunciation. Greek spelling is much more consistent than English. Once you know the rules, you can read any word aloud.
  • Vocabulary. Thousands of English words come from Greek, especially in science, medicine, politics and the arts.
  • Word order. Greek is flexible, but the default order is similar to English, so you can start with simple sentences quickly.
  • Friendly speakers. People in Greece and Cyprus are delighted when you try. You get lots of real practice.

What takes more time

  • Cases. Nouns, adjectives and articles change their endings depending on their job in the sentence. Start with the basics and add detail gradually.
  • Genders. Every noun is masculine, feminine or neuter, and you learn the article with the word.
  • Verb forms. Greek verbs change for person, tense and aspect. Focus on the present tense first.
  • Stress. You need to learn where the stress falls, though the accent mark tells you.
  • Speed. Natural Greek is fast and linked together, so listening practice matters.

How long does it take?

It depends on your goal. Reading the alphabet takes days. Handling polite travel Greek takes a few weeks of regular practice. Having everyday conversations usually takes months of steady lessons. Reaching a high level takes years, as with any language.

The shortcut: speak early. Learners who start talking in their first month, even with just a few phrases, usually progress faster and stay motivated longer than those who only study from books.

A smart way to start

  1. Learn the alphabet and basic sounds (a weekend).
  2. Learn 50 high-frequency words and 20 phrases.
  3. Practise speaking every week with a native tutor.
  4. Add grammar in small pieces as you need it.

That order keeps Greek fun, because you are using it from the start instead of waiting until you feel ready.

Frequently asked questions

Is Greek harder than Spanish for English speakers?

For most English speakers, yes. Spanish shares a lot of vocabulary and uses the same alphabet. Greek has a new alphabet and cases, but consistent pronunciation and many familiar scientific words.

Can I learn Greek by myself?

You can learn the basics alone with free courses and apps. Speaking practice with a native tutor makes progress much faster and builds confidence.

How long does it take to learn Greek?

It depends on your goal. Reading the alphabet takes days, travel Greek takes weeks, everyday conversation takes months of steady practice, and an advanced level takes years.
