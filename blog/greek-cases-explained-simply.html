Greek Cases Explained Simply for Beginners

Greek cases explained simply: nominative, accusative, genitive and vocative with the articles ο, η, το and clear examples for beginners.

By GreekoLearn·28 September 2026·2 min read

In this article

  1. What is a case?
  2. The three articles
  3. One noun, four cases
  4. Where to start
  5. Frequently asked questions

Cases are the part of Greek that scares learners most. They are simpler than their reputation if you learn them in small steps and start with the cases you will use every day.

What is a case?

In English, word order tells you who does what: “The dog sees the cat” is different from “The cat sees the dog”. In Greek, the ending of the word also shows the role. Greek has four cases:

  • Nominative: the subject, the one doing the action.
  • Accusative: the object, and the case after most prepositions.
  • Genitive: belonging, like “of” or ‘s.
  • Vocative: calling or addressing someone.

The three articles

Every noun is masculine, feminine or neuter, and the article shows which one.

GenderNominativeAccusativeExample
Masculineοτονο φίλος (the friend), τον φίλο
Feminineητηνη θάλασσα (the sea), την θάλασσα
Neuterτοτοτο σπίτι (the house), το σπίτι

One noun, four cases

CaseGreekMeaning
NominativeΟ φίλος έρχεται.The friend is coming.
AccusativeΒλέπω τον φίλο.I see the friend.
GenitiveΤο σπίτι του φίλου.The friend’s house.
VocativeΓεια σου, φίλε!Hi, friend!

Where to start

  1. Nominative and accusative first. They cover most everyday sentences.
  2. Learn the article with every noun. Say ο φίλος, not just φίλος.
  3. Memorise prepositions with the accusative. After σε (to, in), από (from), με (with) and για (for), use the accusative: με τον φίλο μου (with my friend).
  4. Add the genitive and vocative later. You will pick them up naturally.

Handy shortcut: σε + τον becomes στον, σε + την becomes στην, and σε + το becomes στο. That is why you hear στο σπίτι (at home) all the time.

Don’t try to memorise tables in one go. Meet each form in real sentences, and the endings start to feel natural.

Frequently asked questions

How many cases does Greek have?

Modern Greek has four cases: nominative, genitive, accusative and vocative.

Do I need to learn all the Greek cases at the start?

No. Start with the nominative and accusative, which cover most everyday sentences, and add the genitive and vocative gradually.

What are the Greek articles?

The definite articles are ο (masculine), η (feminine) and το (neuter). They change form with case and number.
