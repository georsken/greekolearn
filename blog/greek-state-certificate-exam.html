Greek State Certificate Exam: What It Is and How to Prepare

ExamsPublished 24 September 20263 min read

If you want an official record of your Greek, the Greek state certificate is the best-known option. This article explains what it is, what it tests and how to prepare. Always check the official exam website for current dates, fees and rules.

What is the state certificate?

The official certificate for Greek as a foreign or second language is called Πιστοποίηση Ελληνομάθειας (Certificate of Attainment in Greek). It is organised by the Centre for the Greek Language together with the Greek Ministry of Education, and its levels follow the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR), the same scale used for many other European languages, from A1 (beginner) to C2 (mastery). It is not the same as the Greek state certificate for foreign languages such as English or French, which is a different exam system.

Who takes it?

  • People living, studying or working in Greece or Cyprus who need proof of their Greek
  • Heritage learners who want to measure their level
  • Students applying to Greek universities or programmes
  • Anyone who likes the motivation of a clear goal

Some immigration, residence or citizenship processes may ask for a language certificate. Requirements change, so check the official rules for your own situation instead of relying on any blog post.

What does the exam test?

The exam checks all four skills: reading, listening, writing and speaking. Exact formats vary by level, so use the official exam guide and past papers to see what to expect. In general:

SkillTypical tasks
ReadingUnderstanding notices, emails, articles and longer texts, answering questions
ListeningFollowing short dialogues, announcements and interviews
WritingMessages, emails, short compositions, increasing in length with level
SpeakingInteracting with an examiner, describing, giving opinions

How to choose your level

Match your level to the CEFR descriptions. As a rough guide:

  • A1 to A2: you can handle simple everyday exchanges
  • B1 to B2: you can discuss familiar topics and write clear text
  • C1 to C2: you can use Greek flexibly for work and study

It is better to pass at your honest level than to fail one level higher. See how long each level takes.

A preparation plan

  1. Do past papers. Practise under exam conditions and learn the timing and format.
  2. Build vocabulary by theme. Work, health, travel, shopping, education and daily life are common.
  3. Strengthen grammar. Use cases and verb forms accurately, especially in writing.
  4. Practise writing and get feedback. A tutor can mark your texts and show you recurring mistakes.
  5. Rehearse speaking. Practise answering questions and describing pictures aloud, ideally with a native speaker.
  6. Plan your final month. Review weak areas, do a full practice exam each week and rest the day before.
Check official details: exam dates, registration windows, fees and levels offered can change. Confirm everything on the official exam site before you plan.
