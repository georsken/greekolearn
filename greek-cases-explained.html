Greek Cases Explained Simply (Nominative, Accusative, Genitive)

GrammarPublished 22 May 20262 min read

Cases are the part of Greek grammar that scares learners most, but the idea is simple: the ending of a word tells you its job in the sentence. English does this with pronouns (he, him, his). Greek does it with every noun.

The four cases at a glance

CaseJobExample
Nominative (ονομαστική)The subject, the one doing the actionΟ φίλος μιλάει. The friend is talking.
Accusative (αιτιατική)The object, and after most prepositionsΒλέπω τον φίλο. I see the friend.
Genitive (γενική)Possession, “of”, and indirect objectsΤο σπίτι του φίλου. The friend's house.
Vocative (κλητική)Calling or addressing someoneΦίλε, έλα εδώ! Friend, come here!

Articles change too

Greek has three genders and the word for "the" changes with gender and case. Here are the singular forms:

CaseMasculineFeminineNeuter
Nominativeο φίλοςη γυναίκατο παιδί
Genitiveτου φίλουτης γυναίκαςτου παιδιού
Accusativeτον φίλοτη(ν) γυναίκατο παιδί
Vocativeφίλεγυναίκαπαιδί

Notice that for neuter nouns the nominative and accusative are identical, and for many feminine nouns the nominative and accusative look similar too. This lowers the workload a lot.

Prepositions take the accusative

Most common prepositions are followed by the accusative: σε (in, to, at), από (from), για (for), με (with), χωρίς (without).

  • Πάω στο σπίτι. I'm going home. (σε + το becomes στο)
  • Είμαι από την Ελλάδα. I'm from Greece.
  • Μιλάω με τη μητέρα μου. I'm talking with my mother.

Using the genitive

The genitive shows ownership: το βιβλίο του Γιάννη (Yannis's book) and το όνομα της γυναίκας (the woman's name). It is also used for the person receiving something, in the pronoun form: Του δίνω το βιβλίο (I give him the book). You do not need to master all of this at once.

A practical learning order

  1. Start with the nominative and accusative. They cover the vast majority of simple sentences.
  2. Learn nouns with their article. ο δρόμος teaches you the gender automatically.
  3. Add the genitive once you can talk about things belonging to people.
  4. Treat the vocative as a bonus. It is mostly used for names and greetings, such as Γιάννη! or Μαρία!
You will be understood without perfect cases. Greeks understand learners who say “θέλω ο καφές”. Aim for communication first, then polish the endings over time.

Once cases feel less scary, move on to the present tense of Greek verbs. If you want help with grammar, a tutor can give you short, targeted exercises.
